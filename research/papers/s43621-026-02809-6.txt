Leadership competencies: a global bibliometric 
analysis (1960–2025)
Zubir Azhar1 and Ahmad Ayobami1*
1  Introduction
Over the past 65  years, research on leadership competencies has gained substantial 
scholarly attention due to its critical role in shaping organisational performance [1]. The 
development stems from recognising leadership competencies as the set of essential 
skills and capabilities that enable leaders to guide organisations efficiently and effectively 
amid evolving business environment [2–4]. Beyond traditional managerial skills, lead­
ership competencies encompass cognitive, interpersonal, emotional, ethical, and strate­
gic capabilities that allow leaders to anticipate challenges, foster innovation, and align 
organisational objectives with broader economic, societal, and environmental goals. [1].

Abstract
Over the past few decades, research on leadership competencies has expanded 
considerably, shaping both academic inquiries and organisational practice. However, 
its conceptualisation and application remain limited, as many institutions still treat 
leadership competency models as static models rather than evolving capabilities. 
This study bridges the gap through a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of 1,993 
publications indexed in Scopus (1960–2025), examining intellectual foundations, 
publication trends, and thematic evolution. Using BiblioMagika, BiblioMagika Split, 
OpenRefine, and VOSviewer, the study identifies steady growth with over 29,000 
citations across social sciences, business, management, and health domains. The 
United States, the United Kingdom, Australia, and Canada are among the leading 
contributors, with strong international collaboration networks. Results highlight 
theoretical lenses, such as the resource-based view (RBV) and dynamic capabilities, 
alongside emerging themes in leadership competencies. Notably, sustainability 
remains insufficiently embedded within the discourse of leadership competencies, 
indicating a significant avenue for future examination. The study concludes that 
leadership competencies should not be regarded as fixed constructs but as adaptive, 
context-responsive capabilities that evolve with organisational and environmental 
dynamics. These insights provide a roadmap for integrating sustainability, innovation, 
and other contextual factors into future leadership competency models.

Existing literature conceptualises leadership competencies as multidimensional con­
structs that directly influence individual, team, and organisational outcomes. According 
to Ruben et al. [5], leadership competencies comprise an array of skills and capabilities 
that influence team performance. For example, intrapersonal, interpersonal, and cogni­
tive skills are essential for synchronising team efforts and organisational objectives in 
order to achieve a high productivity level [6, 7]. Similarly, technical expertise in one’s 
professional domain fosters a work environment that values teamwork and mutual 
growth. It is also evident that technical competence is essential for managers who want 
to efficiently address workplace challenges and garner the esteem of their subordinates 
[8, 9]. Additionally, emotional and social competencies, including the management of 
interpersonal relationships, are crucial for promoting organisational performance [10, 
11]. Ultimately, these competencies are essential for individuals and organisations in this 
dynamic corporate environment.
Within the contemporary business context, leadership competencies have become 
increasingly indispensable. Shahzad [12] highlights their importance in navigating 
complex and volatile business environments, particularly in integrating technological 
innovations into human resource management systems. Similarly, Natsir et al. [13] dem­
onstrate that leadership competencies, including goal setting, communication, empow­
erment, and mentoring, significantly enhance employee motivation and productivity, 
thereby fostering a positive workplace environment. Hence, enhancing these competen­
cies is imperative not only for individual leadership effectiveness but also for building 
learning organisations capable of sustaining performance amid continuous environmen­
tal change.
Prior studies have emphasised the role of leadership competencies, particularly in 
strengthening communication, collaboration, and strategic decision-making. Rony et 
al. [14] the development of these competencies enhances leadership effectiveness and 
facilitates the achievement of organisational goals. Recent studies also emphasise the 
importance of emotional intelligence, ethical judgment, resilience, and strategic fore­
sight contribute substantially to leadership effectiveness and organisational success 
[15]. For Instance, Boyatzis [16] emotional intelligence attributes result in superior job 
performance. Similarly, Ngayo [17] proposed that leadership competencies comprise 
underlying characteristics, motives, traits, skills, and self-concepts that differentiate high 
performers from average ones. These competencies are intangible assets that provide 
sustained competitive advantage because they are rare, valuable, and difficult to imitate. 
Consequently, leadership competency frameworks are increasingly adopted by organ­
isations and institutions to structure leadership development, performance assessment, 
and succession planning.
However, despite the proliferation of leadership research, the absence of a compre­
hensive integrated knowledge base on leadership competencies limits both theoretical 
progress and practical application. Previous existing reviews are often limited to specific 
disciplines or leadership styles, offering only a partial understanding of the field [18]. 
This conceptual fragmentation impedes the development of unified frameworks that 
could inform leadership education, leadership development, and organisational policy. 
Additionally, the accelerating forces of digital transformation, sustainability imperatives, 
and global crises, including the post-pandemic leadership challenges, underscore the 
need to understand how leadership competencies have evolved and which dimensions

reveal intellectual patterns, research trajectories, and global collaborations, guiding

To address these objectives and bridge the existing conceptual fragmentation, this 
study is guided by the following research questions (RQs):
RQ1  What are the publishing trends, and which journals and countries are the most 
influential contributors to leadership competency research?
RQ2   What are the intellectual and thematic structures of the leadership competency

RQ4  What theoretical frameworks dominate this field?
RQ5  What are the future research opportunities based on recent thematic developments?
This study makes several important contributions. First, it offers a comprehensive and 
systematic mapping of leadership competencies, addressing the long-standing fragmen­
tation through integrating literature from all relevant disciplines. Second, it visualises 
the intellectual structure underpinning this research area through co-citation and biblio­
graphic coupling analyses, illuminating how theories and methodologies have evolved. 
Third, by employing keyword co-occurrence and thematic evolution analysis, the study 
reveals how leadership competency concepts have evolved in response to global chal­
lenges, including sustainability and various forms of diversity. Fourth, through content 
analysis, it identifies the major theoretical frameworks that dominate this field. Finally, 
the study highlights emerging research opportunities that present various promising 
areas. Collectively, these contributions provide a strategic roadmap for scholars, prac­
titioners, and educators seeking to advance leadership theory and practice in a rapidly 
changing world.
1.1  Theoretical background
From a theoretical perspective, both the resource-based view (RBV) and the dynamic 
capabilities theory provide a strong foundation for understanding the strategic value of 
leadership competencies. According to the RBV, leaders’ competencies and capabilities 
serve as intangible assets that shape strategic decisions and organisational outcomes [2]. 
Similarly, from the perspective of the dynamic capabilities theory, competencies can be 
seen as intangible, firm-specific assets that enhance the organisation’s capacity to inte­
grate, build, and reconfigure resources in response to external changes [20].
Within these theoretical lenses, leadership competencies constitute a critical intangi­
ble asset that contributes to strategic competitive advantage through fostering employee 
engagement, ethical conduct, and learning orientation. Moreover, previous studies 
have also linked leadership competencies with corporate responsibility, arguing that

competent leaders play a crucial role in fostering responsible governance [21]. Conse­
quently, leadership competencies are now viewed not merely as personal attributes but 
as strategic imperatives that underpin corporate governance, ethical governance, and 
long-term performance.
Figure 1 conceptualizes leadership competencies as a dynamic configurational system 
that continuously adapts to changing environmental conditions rather than functioning 
as a static or linear set of traits. At the outer layer, environmental dynamics, including 
regulatory changes, market volatility, technological disruption, institutional pressures, 
and sustainability demand, act as ongoing triggers that shape leadership demands and 
organizational priorities. At the core of the framework lies the Leadership Competency 
Configuration System, which represents an integrated and interdependent bundle of 
leadership competencies.
These competencies are not deployed in isolation, instead, they are reconfigured and 
recombined in response to environmental signals. Through this system, leaders actively 
orchestrate and reconfigure organizational resources, capabilities, and routines, consis­
tent with the logic of dynamic capabilities theory. The framework further emphasizes 
strategic reinforcement and feedback, whereby organizational outcomes inform learn­
ing, capability development, and subsequent reconfiguration of leadership competen­
cies. As a result, outcomes such as sustainable performance, competitive advantage, 
organizational resilience, and long-term value creation emerge as dynamic conse­
quences of effective leadership configuration rather than as fixed endpoints. Overall, the 
figure illustrates leadership competencies as a self-adjusting system that co-evolves with 
the environment and organizational performance, providing a robust theoretical foun­
dation for understanding leadership effectiveness under conditions of uncertainty and 
sustainability change.

2  Literature review
Several prior studies attempted to review leadership-related research through narra­
tive or bibliometric methods, yet notable limitations persist. For example, studies have 
conducted a narrative review of leadership, examining 141 articles from the Web of Sci­
ence (WoS) database, covering the period from 2000 to 2022 [22]. Similarly, some have 
also performed a bibliometric review using the Scopus database, analysing 624 scholarly 
works published between 1966 to 2023, with a specific focus on strategic management 
through competencies [23]. Additionally, Tao et al. conducted a bibliometric analysis 
of leadership competencies, reviewing 416 articles from the WoS Core Collection pub­
lished prior to 2022 [24]. However, this study will build upon previous studies by exam­
ining leadership competencies across multiple interdisciplinary fields using the Scopus 
database.
This bibliometric study stands apart from previous research studies in multiple ways. 
The data extraction interval spanned from 1960 to October 10, 2025, encompassing 
1993 publications, which exceeded the parameters of previous inquiries, as it focused 
on global insights into leadership competencies. Second, it concentrates exclusively on 
overarching leadership competencies and the broader spectrum of dimensions, in con­
trast to the narrative review conducted by [22], which solely examines digital leadership 
competencies, and the bibliometric analysis by [23], which scrutinised strategic trends 
in leadership competence. Third, it broadens the parameters of the bibliometric inves­
tigation published by Tao et al. [24] by analysing leadership competency publications 
across all academic disciplines, rather than confining itself to a singular field (Business) 
[24]. Fourth, this research provides a broader view of leadership competencies by using 
a unique database called Scopus, rather than the previous databases utilised in earlier 
bibliometric studies in this leadership area. Lastly, this study provides an overview of 
global publications, an approach that has been overlooked by most past reviews across 
all disciplines.
3  Research methodology
3.1  Search strategy
The data utilised in this research were retrieved from the Scopus repository on October 
10, 2025. The search was conducted for the period 1960–2025, using an expanded set of 
title-based keywords to ensure both precision and conceptual inclusiveness. Specifically, 
the search query applied was: TITLE (“leadership competencies" OR "leadership skill" 
OR “leadership capabilities*” OR “managerial competencies*” OR “managerial skill*” OR 
“managerial capabilities*” OR “executive competencies*” OR "executive skill*” OR “exec­
utive capabilities*”).
This broader query was designed to capture variations in terminology commonly used 
in leadership and management research, ensuring that related constructs were not over­
looked. The restriction to the title field was intentional to guarantee that retrieved pub­
lications directly reflect the centrality of leadership or managerial competencies within 
their scope, minimizing irrelevant records where such terms appear only tangentially in 
abstracts or keywords.
Also. It encompassed details about subject classification, document categorisation, 
source typology, linguistic attributes, geographical location, authors’ institutional affili­
ations, citation metrics, and keywords. Scopus was chosen as the primary data source

3.2  Data collection
The exploration encompassed all articles titled "Leadership Competencies." We incorpo­
rated publications accessible in all languages from 1960 to October 10, 2025, leading to 
1993 publications. The review period (1960–2025) was not predetermined but derived 
from the inclusion and exclusion criteria applied during screening, with the earliest rel­
evant publication appearing in 1960 and the most recent in 2025, reflecting the natural 
span of available literature in the domain [28, 29].
The article titles, abstracts, and keywords of the dataset were scrutinised. In the case 
of uncertainty, the complete text was reviewed. Afterwards, 1993 publications were pro­
cured for examination. This procedure is elucidated in Search strategy.
3.3  Data cleaning and harmonization
Data cleaning and refinement are crucial phases in bibliometric review, as they guar­
antee the precision and reliability of results. This process entails correcting errors, 
removing duplicates, and standardising data formats to create a consistent dataset. By 
thoroughly preparing the data, researchers can effectively apply various bibliometric 
methods, leading to meaningful insights and trends in the scientific literature [30]. In 
the context of this review, we employed OpenRefine, BiblioMagika, and BiblioMagika 
Split. These three advanced tools were designed to clean and harmonise unstructured 
data, including author identities, institutional affiliations, keywords, and other critical 
bibliographic details. Given the diversity of research outputs and potential discrepancies 
inherent in the dataset, this software facilitates data accuracy and consistency.
The data cleaning process started by extracting the Scopus dataset in CSV format. 
Next, the necessary files were created, focusing on specific columns such as keywords, 
author names, and affiliations. Various methods and functions offered by the clustering 
tools were then employed to refine these elements. OpenRefine proved invaluable for 
this task, significantly enhancing data standardisation and accuracy.
BiblioMagika was utilised to conduct comprehensive bibliometric analyses, cover­
ing metrics such as Total Publications (TP), Number of Contributing Authors (NCA), 
Number of Cited Publications (NCP), Total Citations (TC), Citations per Paper (C/P), 
Citations per Cited Paper (C/CP), Citations per Author (C/A), Authors per Paper (A/P), 
Citations per Year (C/Y), Citable Year, h-index, g-index, m-index, and Citation Sum 
within h-Core across different time frames, source titles, authors, institutions, and coun­
tries. Additionally, BiblioMagika enhanced our ability to identify discrepancies in data, 
allowing us to systematically address these issues and perform the necessary data clean­
ing and harmonisation procedures.
After the first cleaning phase, all screened and modified keywords underwent a metic­
ulous manual review to ensure accuracy. Multivalued cells were merged, and the original 
delimiters used in the segmentation process were reinstated to maintain data consis­
tency. Ultimately, the refined and standardised dataset was restored to its original for­
mat to prepare it for further analytical tasks. By utilising the capabilities of these tools, 
we successfully validated our analyses and ensured the reliability of our findings. The 
harmonisation and cleaning processes also made our dataset more detailed and easier

3.4  Data analysis and tools
The data analysis was systematically organised to address the research inquiries outlined 
in the introduction section. Our approach includes capturing the current state of leader­
ship competencies research by examining variables such as document type, source type, 
languages, subject areas, and citation metrics. The findings are shown based on different 
criteria, including annual publication counts, contributions from leading authors, insti­
tutions, countries, and source titles, to identify key contributors and emerging trends 
within the field. We employed bibliometric indicators, including total publications, num­
ber of cited papers, total citations, citations per paper, citations per cited paper, h-index, 
g-index, m-index, and citation sum within the h-core, to thoroughly assess the impact 
and significance of the identified publications.
We applied visualisation techniques to reveal key themes and concepts within the 
field, such as co-occurrence network analysis, thematic mapping, and factorial analysis 
of author keywords. These visual tools enabled us to pinpoint groups of interconnected 
topics, reveal concealed patterns, and obtain a deeper understanding of subdomains 
within the research landscape.
The included studies were analyzed using BiblioMagika, BiblioMagika Split, Open­
Refine, and VOSviewer. Initial data organisation was performed using BiblioMagika. 
Bibliometrics played a pivotal role in converting Scopus metadata into diverse metrics, 
facilitating the cleaning of authors’ data, affiliations, and the associated countries. Sub­
sequently, OpenRefine was used to clean and standardize author names and affiliations. 
After data cleaning and harmonization, network analysis visualizations were conducted 
using Bibliometrix and VOSviewer. VOS viewer, with its graphical interface, was instru­
mental in generating and displaying maps for visualising co-authorship and co-word 
analyses, as well as exploring the evolution of research themes in leadership competen­
cies. Lastly, Biblioshiny, a shiny app linked to the Bibliometrix R package, supported fac­
torial analysis utilising author keywords.
4  Analysis and findings
4.1  Current state
The citation pattern of publications on leadership competencies was carried out using a 
dataset of 1,993 publications spanning from 1960 to 2025 (Table 1). The findings revealed 
that 70.5% (n = 1,404) of these publications were cited at least once. These cited publica­
tions received 29,204 citations, averaging approximately 449.29 citations per year. On a 
per-paper basis, the average citation count was 14.65, while the average for cited papers 
alone was higher, at 20.80. The analysis highlighted an extensive collaborative effort in 
leadership competencies, with an average of 2.72 authors per paper and 5,423 contribut­
ing authors. This level of collaboration indicates a high degree of multi-authorship, sug­
gesting that the field benefits from a diverse range of scholarly contributions.
The h-index for leadership competency publications was calculated at 75, indicating 
that 75 papers in this dataset have each been cited at least 75 times. This metric, which 
measures cumulative impact, aligns well with the field’s sustained scholarly engagement 
over several decades [31]. Furthermore, the g-index was 134, reflecting the significant

The citation analysis of the most influential publications on leadership competencies

performance. It has profoundly influenced both strategic management and leader­
ship research, serving as a conceptual anchor for examining managerial adaptability in 
dynamic environments.

tegic role of managers in driving organisational change and learning. Likewise, Mum­
ford et al. [38] in The Leadership Quarterly offer a classic contribution titled Leadership 
Skills for a Changing World: Solving Complex Social Problems, which has received 586 
citations (22.54 per year). Their research bridges the fields of cognitive psychology and

5  Discussion
This research examined the evolution, intellectual structure, and thematic trends in 
leadership competency research from 1960 to 2025 using an interdisciplinary bibliomet­
ric review of 1993 publications indexed in Scopus. The results indicate that leadership 
competency research has evolved from focusing on individual traits to a comprehensive 
framework linking leadership attributes with organizational performance. This evolution 
also highlights the increasing interdisciplinarity of the field, as scholars more frequently 
incorporate viewpoints from other disciplines.
The analysis for RQ2 and RQ4 revealed that the field of leadership competencies is 
primarily anchored in several theoretical foundations, which include transformational 
leadership theory, human capital theory, dynamic capabilities, and the RBV. Transfor­
mational leadership theory, introduced by Burns and expanded by Bass, emphasises 
competencies such as vision, influence, and motivation that act as mechanisms for 
organisational change [3]. Human capital theory, articulated by Becker, explains that 
leadership competencies are an investment in skills and knowledge that produce eco­
nomic and social benefits. [34]. RBV scholars, such as Barney and Wernerfelt, view these 
competencies as valuable, rare, and inimitable resources that support sustained organi­
zational advantage. [2]. Together, these perspectives frame leadership competencies as a 
behavioural mechanism and a strategic asset for value creation. (Tables 9 and 10).
Building upon these theoretical foundations, this study contributes to the develop­
ment of theory by proposing an integrative lens through which leadership competen­
cies can be understood as an adaptive and context-sensitive system. Rather than viewing 
leadership competencies as static skills, this study positions them as evolving configu­
rations that interact dynamically with organizational strategy, culture, and sustainabil­
ity goals. This aligns with Homer and Lim’s [35] call to advance theory in a globalized 
world by bridging “doing as the Romans do” with “understanding why the Romans do it.” 
Accordingly, the interdisciplinary convergence observed across all literatures highlights 
that leadership competencies are not only behavioural mechanisms but also theoretical 
bridges linking individual effectiveness to organizational performance.
Furthermore, emerging research is increasingly integrating several clusters by link­
ing leadership competencies to measurable performance outcomes and organizational 
growth. Our keyword evolution analysis (RQ3) reveals a significant shift in the field’s. 
Earlier studies (1960s–1990s) predominantly examined leadership skills and manage­
rial effectiveness, whereas recent research (since 2015) emphasizes dynamic leadership, 
digital competencies, ethical decision-making, and sustainability-oriented leadership. 
The growing frequency of keywords such as "emotional intelligence,” "sustainability,” and 
“leadership development” signifies the field’s evolution from static competency frame­
works toward more dynamic, context-responsive models. This shift suggests that schol­
ars are now exploring how leadership competencies evolve under various environmental 
pressures, such as technological disruption, globalisation, and societal expectations, 
rather than merely establishing what competencies leaders should possess.
A key finding from RQ1 is the expanding geographic and institutional diversification 
of leadership competency research. Early contributions were dominated by scholars

Our thematic and intellectual analyses further highlight several underexplored

underrepresented regions and interdisciplinary intersections. By doing so, scholars can 
advance a more holistic understanding of leadership competencies capable of addressing 
the demands of complex, globalised, and ethically sensitive organisational environments.
6  Conclusion
This study provides a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of leadership competency 
research spanning over five decades (1960–2025), based on publications indexed in the 
Scopus database. The findings reveal a steady growth and diversification in this field, 
marked by an increasing number of interdisciplinary contributions and the growing 
involvement of scholars from emerging economies such as China, Malaysia, and South 
Africa. These trends reflect the maturation of leadership competencies from an individ­
ual skill-based construct to a multidimensional framework encompassing behavioural, 
strategic, and ethical dimensions of leadership. Our co-occurrence and thematic anal­
yses identified eight dominant research clusters. Collectively, these clusters define the 
intellectual structure of the field and demonstrate how leadership competencies have 
evolved to address the challenges of dynamic, digitalized, and globalized organizational 
environments. The keyword evolution further indicates a gradual shift in conceptual 
focus from traditional managerial effectiveness to contemporary themes, including digi­
tal transformation, ethical leadership, and sustainability.
The findings also reveal that transformational leadership theory, human capital theory, 
dynamic capabilities, and the resource-based view (RBV) are the prevailing theoretical 
foundations underpinning research on leadership competency. These frameworks con­
ceptualize leadership competencies as both behavioural enablers and strategic resources 
that drive innovation, adaptability, and long-term organizational success. Together, they 
reinforce the notion that leadership competencies should not be viewed as peripheral 
Page 29 of 31
Azhar and Ayobami Discover Sustainability           (2026) 7:638 
attributes but as essential, value-creating assets that determine an organisation’s capacity 
to compete and sustain performance in complex environments.
From a practical standpoint, this study highlights that leadership competencies serve 
as a bridge between personal capability and organisational resilience. For managers and 
policymakers, investing in competency development, particularly in emotional intelli­
gence, digital agility, and sustainability-oriented skills, can foster leadership cultures that 
promote ethical decision-making, innovation, and inclusive growth.
Lastly, this research identifies several promising avenues for future inquiry. These 
include examining the role of digital technologies and AI in leadership competency 
development, assessing the impact of cultural and institutional contexts on competency 
frameworks, exploring the integration of sustainability and ethical dimensions into lead­
ership models, and employing longitudinal and mixed-method designs to trace compe­
tency evolution over time. Such directions can deepen theoretical integration, enhance 
global relevance, and contribute to a more nuanced understanding of how leadership 
competencies shape contemporary organisational success.
Overall, this study affirms that leadership competencies have evolved into a strategic 
and interdisciplinary domain central to organisational sustainability and performance. 
By aligning theoretical insights with empirical trends, future research can continue to 
advance this field, bridging the gap between leadership theory, competency develop­
ment, and practical application in an increasingly complex and interconnected world.
7  Limitations
While this study offers valuable insights into the intellectual and thematic landscape of 
leadership competency research, several limitations have been acknowledged. First, the 
analysis relied exclusively on the Scopus database, which may have excluded relevant 
studies published in other repositories. Future research could enhance the comprehen­
siveness of this field by integrating multiple databases, such as Web of Science (WoS), 
Google Scholar, or regional databases. Second, the title-based search strategy, while 
ensuring conceptual precision, may have omitted studies addressing leadership compe­
tencies under alternative terminologies. Expanding future searches to include abstracts 
and keywords could help capture broader conceptual variations and improve inclusive­
ness. Third, this study employed Open Refine, VOSviewer, and BiblioMagika Split as the 
main analytical tools, which, despite their robustness, may limit analytical depth in areas 
such as thematic evolution or conceptual network dynamics. Future studies could com­
bine or triangulate these tools with Cite Space, Biblioshiny, or SciMAT to achieve a more 
nuanced visualization and temporal mapping of research trends. Finally, while this study 
provides a bibliometric overview, it does not empirically test the proposed theoretical 
integrations between leadership competencies, organizational culture, and sustainable 
corporate performance. Future research should empirically validate these relationships 
through a quantitative model (e.g., structural equation model, SEM, or partial least 
squares structural equation model, PLS-SEM) or qualitative inquiry (e.g., case studies or 
in-depth interviews) to strengthen theoretical generalizability.

Title: Data-Informed, Not Data-Driven
Authors: Karim Ragab
Source: Crossref
Date: 2026-2-20
URL: https://doi.org/10.4018/979-8-3373-4908-4.ch011

Abstract (EN):
As artificial intelligence tools increasingly shape instructional delivery, student monitoring, and administrative operations in K–12 education, school leaders must navigate the fine line between leveraging data for insight and over-relying on algorithmic outputs. This chapter critically examines the evolving role of educational leaders in Gulf and Middle Eastern school systems, where ambitious national AI strategies intersect with traditional education governance and cultural expectations. It argues for a data-informed leadership model, one that integrates AI-generated insights into human-centered, pedagogically sound decision-making processes. The chapter unpacks the risks of data determinism, including eroded teacher agency, student dehumanization, and biased performance metrics. It introduces actionable strategies and transparency frameworks that restore judgment, context, and trust to leadership practices. The chapter positions AI not as a substitute for educational vision but as a tool that must be curated, interpreted, and ethically managed by reflective school leaders.
